Project 4: Top Spinner

September 21, 2026

At a glance

Goal: Design and build a spinning top that could spin the longest.

Result: The top lasted around 13 seconds.

Key takeaway: A compound gear train can convert a strong motor's torque into speed, with a combined speed increase of about 13x.

The Challenge

This project is inspired by Beyblades, but it was also an opportunity to understand gear ratios. The goal of this project was to design and build a spinning top that could spin the longest.

Constraint: The strongest motor in the LEGO SPIKE Prime kit delivers strong torque but lacks speed.

Design Process

Final design: Compound gear train

I used the strongest motor provided in the LEGO SPIKE Prime kit. While this motor delivers strong torque, it lacks speed. To create a top that spins fast, I implemented a compound gear train to convert that torque into speed.

The train uses a 36-tooth gear as the input, driving a 10-tooth gear. That 10-tooth gear shares an axle with a second 36-tooth gear, which in turn drives a final 10-tooth output gear. Each stage steps up the speed by a factor of 3.6, giving a combined speed increase of about 13x.

Stage Driver → Driven Speed increase
1 36-tooth → 10-tooth 3.6x
2 36-tooth → 10-tooth 3.6x
Total ≈ 13x

Results

The top lasted around 13 seconds. (see Demo Video)
