Project 5: Spirographs

September 27, 2026

Spirographs are drawings consisting of curves generated by a point on a circle rolling inside another circle. These intricate patterns are called hypotrochoids and epitrochoids. Instead of circles inside of each other, we can use motors to mathematically model this motion.

There was no difficulty designing the model of my spirograph, but I wasn't too satisfied with my initial results (not documented). The drawings weren't quite as intricate and artistic as I had hoped. I thought the reason for this was the design of the arm — it makes a sweeping motion, but its path of trajectory isn't close to the center of the rotating base. I still continued with my design, because I could alter the speed and behavior of the motors.

In trials 1 and 2, both motors were spun at different rates. The difference is that I swapped their speeds with each other in the second trial.

In trials 3 and 4, I set the speeds of the motors to be integer multiples of each other. I also swapped their speeds in trial 4. What I created, accidentally, in trial 4 was an inner-loop limaçon.

In the final pair of trials (5 and 6), I altered the behavior of the arm. Its motion is no longer continuous — it pauses briefly every 0.25 seconds. The results I got were interesting.

Findings: I think that having the arm's speed faster than the rotating base's produces more artistic designs, at least with my spirograph model. In the trials where the rotating base was significantly faster than the arm, I noticed patterns of "dots," though I believe that effect is from the Lego panel underneath. When the speeds of the arm and the base are integer multiples of each other, they no longer produce circles in the long run.

Motor behavior code:

# Setup: motors are connected to ports D and E.
from hub import port
import runloop
import motor

# Choose a speed for each motor, in degrees per second.
# Change these values independently; use a negative number to reverse direction.
BASE_SPEED = 200
ARM_SPEED = 1000

async def motion_one():
    # Start both motors without waiting, so they run at the same time.
    motor.run(port.D, ARM_SPEED)
    motor.run(port.E, BASE_SPEED)

    # Let the motors run for fifty seconds.
    await runloop.sleep_ms(500000)

    # Stop both motors after the five-second run.
    motor.stop(port.D)
    motor.stop(port.E)
  
async def motion_two():
    # Start the base motor continuously.
    motor.run(port.E, BASE_SPEED)

    # Keep cycling the arm: move for 0.25 seconds, then pause for 0.25 seconds.
    while True:
        await motor.run_for_time(port.D, 250, ARM_SPEED)
        await runloop.sleep_ms(250)
