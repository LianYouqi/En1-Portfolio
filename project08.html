Project 8: Astronaut Tools

Description of this project: The goal of this project is to design a tool that can screw and unscrew bolts off of a screw. The twist is that the tool must be able to work in a zero-gravity environment. Some of the constraints outlined by the class includes working with gloves, the bolts must be able to be held by the tool, and it can work upside down (to micmic the conditions in space) My initial design were to construct a contraption that uses rubber bands as a way to unscrew the bolts. The reason for this is that the bolts are shaped hexagonal and the rubber bands can be stretched to fit around the bolts. The speed at which the rubber bands spins can also be adjusted with gear ratios. I went through two iterations of this idea. [picture of first iteration] At this point, I wanted to try to increase the speed by increasing the gear ratios because my design weren't that fast compared to the other designs I've seen from my classmates [picture of second iteration] I found that it was challenging to implement gear ratios for the first iteration. So, I changed the design with two motors on one side of the tool. My thoughts were that I've effectively doubled the torque, and this configuration will be easier to implement the gear ratios that I wanted. It did not work out as I expected, and I could not bulid the highest possible gear ratio that I had in mind. Even then, I've found that the bolts were continuously screwed on or off the bolt when I recorded in slow-motion. This was actually the issue that I wanted to avoid in the first place, because I thought I could apply tension to the string to unscrew the nut in a continuous motion. Another problem that I didn't consider yet was how to implement a way to hold the bolt in place after the tool unscrews the bolt as per the instructions of this project. In short, I moved on to another iteration because implementing a way to hold the bolt wasn't feasible with my current design. Also, the unscrewing procress was not effective. Which leads to my third iteration. At this point, despite not wanting to take 'inspiration' from my classmates, I decided to make drill-like design with four lego supports as both a way to screw and hold the bolt. I obviously added in my own touch: two motors that provide extra torque, and high gear ratios. The construction of the gear ratios were the hardest part of this iteraiton because my design were to transfer the motion of the gears between axes in different directions. I've used lego parts that you mainly associate with buliding models in order to get the alignment right, as well as rubber bands to hold the gears in place.
