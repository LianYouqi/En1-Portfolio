Project 8: Astronaut Tools

At a glance

Goal: Design a tool that can screw and unscrew bolts in a zero-gravity environment.

Result: A drill-like design with four LEGO supports to screw and hold the bolt, driven by two motors with high gear ratios.

Key takeaway: The rubber-band designs could not hold the bolt or unscrew it effectively, which led to the drill-style design.

The Challenge

The goal of this project is to design a tool that can screw and unscrew bolts off of a screw. The twist is that the tool must be able to work in a zero-gravity environment. Some of the constraints outlined by the class include working with gloves, the bolts must be able to be held by the tool, and it can work upside down to mimic the conditions in space.

Design Process

Iteration 1: Rubber bands

My initial design was to construct a contraption that uses rubber bands as a way to unscrew the bolts. The reason for this is that the bolts are shaped hexagonally, and the rubber bands can be stretched to fit around the bolts. The speed at which the rubber bands spin can also be adjusted with gear ratios. I went through two iterations of this idea.

At this point, I wanted to try to increase the speed by increasing the gear ratios because my design was not that fast compared to the other designs I had seen from my classmates.

Iteration 1

Iteration 2: Two motors on one side

I found that it was challenging to implement gear ratios for the first iteration. So, I changed the design with two motors on one side of the tool. My thoughts were that I had effectively doubled the torque, and this configuration would be easier to implement the gear ratios that I wanted. It did not work out as I expected, and I could not build the highest possible gear ratio that I had in mind. Even then, I found that the bolts were continuously screwed on or off the bolt when I recorded in slow motion. This was actually the issue that I wanted to avoid in the first place, because I thought I could apply tension to the string to unscrew the nut in a continuous motion.

Another problem that I had not considered yet was how to implement a way to hold the bolt in place after the tool unscrews the bolt, as required by the project instructions. In short, I moved on to another iteration because implementing a way to hold the bolt was not feasible with my current design. Also, the unscrewing process was not effective.

Iteration 2

Iteration 3 (Final design): Drill-style with four supports

This led to my third iteration. At this point, despite not wanting to take inspiration from my classmates, I decided to make a drill-like design with four LEGO supports as both a way to screw and hold the bolt. I added my own touch: two motors that provide extra torque and high gear ratios.

Final design, top view

The construction of the gear ratios was the hardest part of this iteration because my design was to transfer the motion of the gears between axes in different directions. I used LEGO pieces typically associated with building models in order to get the alignment right, as well as rubber bands to hold the gears in place. The increments that these LEGO pieces were designed in were not ideal for this iteration.

The final design uses the improved drill-style approach with four support points and dual motors to increase torque while keeping the tool stable during operation.
